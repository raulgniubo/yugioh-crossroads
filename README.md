# Yu-Gi-Oh! Crossroads

A private fan project: a 3D Yu-Gi-Oh! story game with characters from Duel Monsters, GX, 5D's and ZEXAL.
Not affiliated with or endorsed by Konami. Not for distribution.

Built with **Godot 4.7** for macOS and Windows first, iOS and Android later.

## What is in 0.64.0

## What is in 0.63.1

Fixes from the first day of 0.63.0: how the duelists move on the real models, memory, the deck editor, and the sound of a hit. No cards added: 8,415 total.

- **Duelists no longer cover their own faces or cross their arms in an X.** The motions were made on a test body with shorter arms than the real models (theirs are about 45% longer, with bigger hands and heads). Every motion is now fitted to each model's own measurements: hands go where they should relative to that body, and they are kept off the face, both from the front and from the corner camera's side. Checked on the skeletons of all 25 models.
- **Card motions made by hand for each body:** the draw, playing a monster, setting a card, a Spell, a Trap, thinking (looking over the cards, not a hand on the chin), one ace gesture, and the fired-up stance (it was the boxer's guard that showed as crossed arms on the victory screen).
- **Out of duels:** two everyday motions that looked odd on the long arms. Losing a monster or being negated is a step back now, going second is a nod.
- **The card panel no longer covers your duelist.** It ends above their head, and long texts scroll inside it.
- **The live portrait is wider with soft edges,** so a hand at arm's length is not sliced by the frame, and its camera stands further back with a longer lens, so a hand reaching forward is not huge.
- **Pain sounds (Spanish).** A grunt for a small hit, a cry for a big one, a long one for the hit that ends the duel: 48 sounds, 4 for each of the 12 duelist voices. Heard when the hit lands. Same one-voice rule as every line: if somebody is talking it waits a moment, and if they are not done it is skipped. English gets its 48 with the rest of its voices.
- **Quiet after the last hit.** Once a hit takes someone to 0, nobody comments as if the duel went on (Lumen said "they are almost out" after the winning blow). The only words left are the ending's.
- **Memory.** Card pictures used to stay in memory at full size forever, and so did the 3D model behind every duelist picture ever shown. After a few duels and packs that was gigabytes: the world dropped to 15-20 FPS and, on a smaller graphics card, new cards came up as white boxes. Now pictures are kept at the size they are shown at, only the recent ones are kept, and models that nobody is looking at are let go. Tested by playing duels in a row and by opening and closing every big screen and the world several times: memory holds steady.
- **The deck editor opens at once.** It loaded the full-size picture of every card you own before showing anything (600 cards: 8 seconds and 2.6 GB; thousands: minutes). It now loads only the cards near what is on screen, in the background. 5,000 cards: 1 second.
- **Buying a deck adds its cards to yours.** A card you already had one of, and the deck has one of, is now two (up to three).
- **Accumulated Fortune and Chain Strike.** With "Ask me at every chance" off, the game passed for you after your own card, so you could not stack up to Chain Link 4. While you hold one of these, you are asked at every link of a chain. The cards themselves were right (checked with real chains, now a permanent test: `tests/chain_check.tscn`). Every chain is also written to the game's log.
- **Exodia's show (and the other special wins) can't be skipped by accident.** A mouse click or the wheel used to cut it off in the middle. Now only Esc, Enter, Space or a controller button skips it, and not in its first second.
- **Card search knows short names:** FGD (Five-Headed Dragon), BEWD, DMG, BLS and the initials of any card.
- **Windows:** only the .exe changed.

## What is in 0.63.0

Duelists come alive: they move like duelists, the camera goes to them at the big moments, the duel has an ending, and they talk a lot more. No cards added: 8,415 total.

**Spanish has every voice in this build. English still plays with text bubbles** and gets all its voices in a later build, when they are all made.

- **Live duelists in the corners.** The two pictures next to the Life Points are now the real 3D duelists, from the chest up, moving all through the duel. Whatever they do at the table shows there: the stance of their personality, the draw, the card in their hand, the play, the thinking pose, the stagger when a hit lands, the kneel when they lose. When a duelist throws an arm out or lifts a hand high, their little camera steps back by itself so the whole gesture stays in the picture, then moves back in. While they say a line their head moves with the words, and the mouth moves too on models that have a movable face. The table stays exactly as visible as before. Settings > Gameplay > **Live duelists in the corners** turns it off (still pictures, like before).
- **Duelists that move.** Over 40 new motions made for duels: drawing, playing a card, setting one, a Spell, a Trap, pointing an attack in, a direct attack, ending the turn, thinking, three ways of taking a hit, holding the head when the ace falls, and more. Before, a duel borrowed everyday motions (a nod, a chat) and tilted the whole body like one stiff piece.
- **Five personalities.** Kind, cocky, cool, wild and dark duelists each stand their own way (arms crossed, hand in the pocket, bouncing on their feet, head low...), win their own way and lose their own way. People with no personality of their own get one from their name, always the same one.
- **Signature moves.** Each of the 25 duelists you have a model for has their own pair: a pose for the face-off and a gesture for the monster they are known for. The six leads each have a face-off pose nobody else uses. They are moves made for this game, not poses from the shows.
- **Your Style.** Profile > You > **Style**: Pick for me, Friendly, Confident, Cool, Fired up, Dark. It decides how your duelist stands, wins and loses.
- **A card in the hand.** When a duelist draws or plays, there is a card in their hand.
- **They watch the duel.** Both duelists turn their heads to the card being played, the monster attacking and the one being destroyed, then back to each other.
- **The turn beat.** Each turn (from the second on) opens with a moment on the duelist whose turn it is, calling it and drawing. About a second on the first turns, shorter after.
- **The camera at the big moments.** An ace: the duelist first, then the monster. A Trap: a quick look at the one who sprang it. A direct attack: the one who sends it in, then the strike into the other duelist. Never more than one look every 4 seconds.
- **Hits reach the duelist.** Battle damage flies on past the monster that lost and lands on the duelist, with a flash and sparks on the body, and the Life Points drop when it lands.
- **The ending scene.** After the last hit: the loser takes it in, the winner has their moment and says their line, the loser answers, the camera pulls back over the arena, and Lumen has the last word. About 7 seconds; any key or button skips it. Special wins (Exodia, Destiny Board...) keep their own show and then go to a short version.
- **A lot more talking (Spanish).** 626 new spoken lines: your duelist and the opponents call the turn, the draw, going first or second, a summon, a set, a Spell, the battle, a direct attack, the end of the turn and a big hit, each personality in its own words. Your duelist sends 28 famous monsters in by name ("¡Mago Oscuro, ataca!"). Lumen has new lines for who goes first, a long duel, a thin Deck, a close win, a flawless win and a loss. The ending plays the winner's line and then the loser's, yours included. The same line is never said twice in a row.
- **A line that names a card is only said about that card.** Before, each duelist's line for "their monster" played for any big summon, so Kaiba announced "the Blue-Eyes White Dragon" for the three-headed fusion and Zane said "Cyber End Dragon" for any Level 7. Now 34 lines that name a card, a family (Harpies, Toons, Destiny HEROes, Blackwings, Elemental HEROes) or a monster's own attack ("Burst Stream of Destruction!", "Dark Magic Attack!") are tied to exactly that card. Any other big monster gets a line with no name in it. Also fixed: a small monster brought back by an effect was announced like an ace. A test in the project checks every one of these lines against the card list (`tests/line_names.tscn`).
- **Settings > Audio > How much they talk:** Some (the big moments, like 0.62.0), Normal (the calls of a turn too), A lot (every card and every attack).
- **Settings > Gameplay:** **Live duelists in the corners** (on / off), **Camera cuts** (Full / Fewer / Off) and **Turn beat** (Full / Brief / Off).
- **Spanish wording.** The new lines are neutral Latin American Spanish, and 11 of Lumen's older lines that used words from Spain ("Vale", "Menuda", "a por") were reworded and recorded again in her three voices.
- **Voices still never overlap.** Same rule as before, and the test for it still passes.
- **The voice files are inside the game** (1,529 Spanish lines now). Nothing is saved outside the game's folder.
- **Windows:** only the .exe changed.

## What is in 0.60.0

Attacks made of their Attribute, and the Exodia win. No cards added: 8,415 total.

- **Every attack is made of the monster's Attribute now, in three beats.** The element gathers around the monster when it declares the attack, crosses the field with it, and lands on the target, leaving something behind for a moment.
  - **FIRE:** tongues of flame rise around it, a stream of fire crosses the field (the floor under it catches), and it ends in a fireball that cools to smoke, a scorch of glowing embers and, for strong monsters, a pillar of fire.
  - **WATER:** water jumps up around it, a torrent and a wave run across the field, and it crashes in a crown of spray with ripples spreading over the floor. Strong monsters raise a waterspout.
  - **WIND:** a whirlwind winds up around it, lines of air and blades of wind fly ahead, and a tornado stands on the target.
  - **EARTH:** the floor cracks under it, the crack runs across the field throwing up stones, and rocks burst up under the target in a ring with dust.
  - **LIGHT:** a halo opens over it, a lance of light crosses the field, blades of light fall around the target and a column comes down from the sky over a seal.
  - **DARK:** a pool of shadow opens under it, black smoke crawls over the floor, and fangs of shadow come up around the target and close, with dark flames.
  - **DIVINE:** a gold seal and a pillar, lightning across the field, and thunder coming down three times on the target.

From 2400 ATK (or Level 7 and up) it is the big show: the monster holds its pose a beat longer, the camera sits a little further back so all of it fits, and the extras come in (the fire pillar, the waterspout, the second whirlwind). The finishing blow uses the big version too.

- Signature attacks (White Lightning and the rest) and Turbo Duels play as before.
- **Winning with Exodia has its own show** (about 8 seconds, and any key or button skips it). The field goes dark and the music drops. The five pieces fly out of the hand one by one and land, each with a gong, on the points of a gold seal, chained to each other. The chains snap, the cards rise and stand together as one towering figure (head, two arms, two legs), seen from the loser's end looking up. "EXODIA" crosses the screen, the figure gathers light and fires a beam down the whole field, and the picture goes white and breaks like glass. Then the usual win screen. It plays the same way when the opponent does it to you. (Before, the duel just ended with the win screen.)
- **Windows:** only the .exe changed.

## What is in 0.50.0

**The duel runs on the rules engine only, and the way you play a duel got a fresh pass.** No cards were added: the game still has **3,106 cards**. This is the build where `ocgcore` became `main`.

**Playing a card, attacking and choosing things**

- **The menu on a card.** Tap a card and its choices spring out with their own icon and color and a line that says what each one does: Summon (face-up, Attack Position), Tribute Summon, Set, Special Summon, Activate, Use its effect (with the effect's own text), Flip Summon, To Attack or To Defense Position.
- **Drag a card onto the table.** Pick a card up from your hand and the zones it can go to light up. Drop it on the zone you want. If there is one way to play it, it is played there at once; a monster you could Summon or Set opens those two choices right over that zone.
- **You choose the zone.** A monster played from its menu lights up the free zones and you tap one. "Place it for me" (or Esc) puts it in the middle first, like before. Spell and Trap Cards played from the menu are placed for you; dragged ones go where you drop them.
- **Attacks are aimed.** Tap one of your monsters in the Battle Phase: an arrow runs from it to the pointer, their monsters light up red, and their side of the field becomes a "DIRECT ATTACK" target when the attack can go direct. Tap the target, or hold the press, drag onto it and let go. Nothing is declared until you choose, and Esc or a right-click calls it off. In Main Phase 1 you can drag one of your Attack Position monsters onto a target: the Battle Phase starts by itself and the attack is declared.
- **One box to choose cards.** Tributes, materials for a Synchro, Xyz or Fusion Summon, discards, cards from the Deck or a Graveyard: one box, tap a card to choose it (it lifts, gets a green rim and its number), tap it again to take it back, a counter shows how many, and Confirm lights up when the choice is legal. Sums (a Ritual's Levels) show the total so far. With one card to choose, a second tap on it confirms.
- **Cards on the table are chosen on the table.** When every card you can choose is on the field (Tributes for a Tribute Summon, Xyz materials, a target), they get a marker over them, you tap them there, and the line at the top has the counter and Confirm.
- **Attack or Defense.** When a Special Summon lets you choose, the card itself is shown each way it can go and you tap one. Only the positions the rules allow are offered.
- **Wording.** "Activate the effect of X?", "Choose 2 monsters to Tribute", "X: tap the monster it attacks", "Choose a zone for X". The stale note "Not in this game yet: this card plays with its ATK and DEF only" is gone from every card.

**Animations**

- A card you play leaves from where it was in your hand (or where you let go of it), not from the middle of the screen.
- **Summon:** it rises over its zone, the zone lights under it, and it is slammed down with a flash, a ring and sparks. **Set:** it glides in low and is laid face-down with a soft ring.
- **Attack:** the monster leans back like a drawn bow while a red ring closes on its target, lunges (slow off the mark, fastest when it lands), the monster it hits is knocked back, and it recoils before going home. A direct attack goes all the way with a bigger blast.

**Fixed**

- **Tributes left hanging in the air.** Monsters Tributed for a Tribute Summon (three for a Level 10, for example) stayed floating over the table until the turn ended. The engine calls those cards "materials", and the table waited for a Special Summon that never came. They now go to the Graveyard the way Tributes do, as lights that the new monster collects.
- **The opening.** The four worlds and the duelist faces are prepared before the first picture, so there is no stop when the next world comes in, and the worlds are put away one at a time at the end instead of all in one frame. This was fixed by reading the code; the cloud has no graphics card, so the first real look is yours.

**The old duel rules are out of play.** The switch in Settings is gone, Free Duel no longer falls back to them, and the duel screen has no code left that could run them (it went from 2,858 lines to about 1,630). If the rules engine cannot load on a computer, a duel says so instead of starting on the old rules.

**Not done, said plainly**

- **Old rules files still in the repo.** `duel_state.gd`, `effect_runner.gd`, `spell_rules.gd`, `duel_ai.gd`, `card_effects.gd` and `card_exact.gd` are still there. Nothing runs a duel with them, but the table's picture of the duel is built on `duel_state.gd`, and the collection and the Deck Builder read card facts from the others. Taking them out is its own careful job, and it comes later, when Raul says so (no change you can see in the game).
- **Chain replies** ("your opponent activated X, respond?") use the new box, but Spell and Trap zones from the menu, the order of simultaneous effects and counters are still chosen for you.
- **Controller:** aiming an attack and choosing a zone are mouse and touch only for now.
- **Nothing here was seen on a real graphics card.** Every interaction was run and photographed in the cloud (software rendering), and duels were played through automatically with random choices in the new boxes and on the table.

## What is in 0.42.0

**The opponent that thinks ahead.** This is step 2 of the engine move: on the new engine the CPU now has three levels, Normal, Hard and Extreme. No cards added: the game still has **1,998 cards**, every one working and obtainable.

- **Try it:** Free Duel, ENGINE: New (test). The three difficulty buttons there now read Normal, Hard and Extreme.
- **How it thinks:** before it answers, it copies the duel, gives each answer it could give on a copy, plays the next turns out, and scores where that leaves it. The real card scripts run in every one of those tries, so it works for every card, including ones nobody wrote any rules of thumb for.
- **It does not cheat:** it never reads your hand, your Deck or your face-down cards. Each try re-deals everything it cannot see (a different random deal each time), so it plans against what you might have, not what you do have. That also covers coin tosses and its own next draws.
- **The levels:** Normal looks a little ahead, tries each answer against 2 deals, and now and then takes its second-best idea. Hard tries each answer against up to 6 deals. Extreme goes up to 12, and for its best ideas also looks one more decision ahead. It thinks on its own threads, so the screen never freezes; "is thinking..." shows when it takes more than a moment.
- **No more handicap on the new engine:** the classic Hard gives the CPU an extra starting card. On the new engine it does not get one. Its edge is how it plays. It still brings the tuned deck on Hard and Extreme.
- **ygo-agent (the outside AI) is not used.** It only knows the cards on its own list and it runs as a Python server, so it cannot play our decks inside the game. The opponent here is our own.
- **Under the hood:** `native/ai.hpp` (the search), one addition to the engine (`OCG_DuelDeterminize`, the fair re-deal), scripts are compiled once so a copy of a duel starts in 5 ms instead of 17.

How it measured (all on the new engine, decks paired so each side plays both decks):

- Hard against the step 1 placeholder: **151 wins, 48 losses, 1 draw in 200 duels (75.5%)**. My bar was 80%. It did not reach it. For scale: a version that was allowed to see every hidden card only reached 70% before the last improvements, so a lot of what is left is the luck of the draw.
- Normal against the placeholder: 45 wins, 34 losses, 1 draw in 80 duels (56%).
- Extreme against Hard: 20 wins, 18 losses in 38 duels. Too close to call. On my test machine both get about the same number of tries per decision; Extreme pulls ahead of Hard only when the computer gives it more of them (it uses up to 4 threads on yours).
- Test boards (30 set positions: a win on the table, an attack that would be a mistake, a card that should or should not be played): Hard 30 of 30, Extreme 30 of 30, Normal 28 of 30. The placeholder gets 27 of 30, so these boards mostly prove it does not do dumb things, not that it is brilliant.
- Thinking time on my slow test machine, one thread: the longest single decision was 1.8 seconds on Hard.

## Playing a build

## Running from the editor

1. Install [Godot 4.7](https://godotengine.org/download) (standard version, not .NET).
2. Open Godot, click Import, pick this folder's `project.godot`.
3. Press F5.

## Real card images (optional)

The game runs with generated placeholder cards. To use real card images, run the downloader and point it at the
game's card folder (default `~/Documents/Crossroads/assets`, changeable in Settings):

```
python3 tools/fetch_cards.py --set "Legend of Blue Eyes White Dragon" --set "Metal Raiders" --out ~/Documents/Crossroads/assets
```

Character pictures (Yugi, Kaiba, Joey, Jaden, Yusei, Yuma) work the same way:

```
python3 tools/fetch_characters.py --out ~/Documents/Crossroads/assets
```

They land in `characters/<key>.png`. Drop your own `player.png` there to use your own picture.
Card and character images are never committed to this repo.
